\subsection{Sinhala-ABD}
\label{sec:abd-construction}

\paragraph{Source passages and labels.}
Mixed documents are built from contiguous Wikipedia passages of six to twelve
sentences, cut from articles that survive a page-type and prose filter
(Appendix~\ref{app:abd-cleaning}). Every sentence carries a human or machine
label, and boundaries are derived from the label sequence, so a boundary is an
index where the label changes. Localising a change of authorship inside largely
human text is a stricter setting than document-level classification
\citep{wang2024semeval,zeng2024aaai}.

\paragraph{Construction types and composition.}
Figure~\ref{fig:abd-constructions} illustrates continuation, single-span
replacement, and multi-span replacement; Table~\ref{tab:abd-composition}
summarises the corpus by construction type and generator. \emph{Continuation}
follows RoFT \citep{dugan2022roft}: a human prefix is followed by machine text,
with the split sampled uniformly between 20\% and 80\% of the passage. The
replacement types instead place machine text inside human text, as in hybrid
essays \citep{zeng2024aaai,gao2024llm}: single-span rewrites one internal span
of one to three sentences, and multi-span rewrites two or three non-adjacent
spans of one to two sentences, each generated independently from the original
passage so that no consistent machine register spans the document. Spans never
include the first or last sentence and are separated by at least one human
sentence; with the 20--80\% sampling, this keeps position from identifying a
boundary (Figure~\ref{fig:abd-positions}). Replacement rather than insertion
keeps the surrounding text genuinely human-written and holds topic constant
\citep{wang2024m4}, and spans are length-matched to the text they replace, since
length alone otherwise becomes a shortcut \citep{doughman2024limitations}.
Gemini~2.5~Pro is generated for a disjoint subset of articles so that it can be
held out entirely \citep{macko2023multitude}. Prompts are reproduced in
Appendix~\ref{app:abd-prompts}.

\paragraph{Automatic validation of Sinhala-ABD.}
Because a mixed document's labels are only correct if the generated span is
usable in place of the text it replaced, every generation passes seven automatic
checks: exact sentence count; word count within $\pm$25\% of the replaced span;
no preamble; no Markdown; predominantly Sinhala script; not a near-copy of the
original span; and preservation of numeric tokens, so a date or quantity cannot
silently mutate. A failing generation is retried up to three times and then
discarded, and outputs are never edited by hand. Across 5{,}109 rejected
generations, which record 5{,}951 individual check failures since one generation
may fail several checks, 53.7\% of failures were length violations, 23.9\%
sentence-count violations, 16.3\% number mutations, 3.5\% script violations,
2.3\% near-copies, and 0.3\% formatting. The length tolerance was deliberately
not relaxed: all three generators under-write Sinhala relative to the human
span, and admitting shorter machine spans would let length substitute for
authorship \citep{doughman2024limitations}. Cleaning is symmetric and minimal,
since surface form itself carries authorship information
\citep{mcgovern2024fingerprints}. 76.8\% of accepted documents passed on the
first attempt, 15.2\% after one retry, and 8.0\% after two or more.
