\section{Validation}
\label{sec:validation}

\noindent\textit{Validation status. Preliminary manual reading suggested that
the generated Sinhala texts in both datasets were coherent and generally
preserved the intended meaning. These observations are informal; systematic
evaluations have not yet been conducted, and results remain pending. The
human-study sample sizes below are planned targets. Automatic-validation
figures are measured.}

\paragraph{Native-speaker quality.}
Two native Sinhala speakers will independently assess the same 600 generated
texts. For Sinhala-HAT, 450 texts: 150 per generator, comprising 50 each from
news, Wikipedia, and social media. For Sinhala-ABD, a further 150 machine spans,
50 per generator, drawn across the three construction types. Blinded to the
generator, they will give separate yes/no judgements of fluency (natural,
readable Sinhala) and meaningfulness (understandable, coherent content). ABD
spans are judged in place, with their surrounding human context visible, and
carry a third judgement: whether the span preserves the facts of the text it
replaced. For each generator and criterion, we will report the mean of the two
assessors' percentages of yes ratings, together with agreement and disagreement
counts.

\paragraph{Human detection baseline.}
Ten native Sinhala speakers will each complete both tasks. For Sinhala-HAT they
will classify 90 texts as human- or AI-written, with 45 texts per class: 30
texts per domain, comprising 15 human and 15 AI, with five AI texts from each
generator. For Sinhala-ABD they will additionally mark, in each of 10 mixed
documents, which sentences they believe are machine-written. True labels will be
hidden, no participant will see both members of a human--AI pair, and
participants will receive no training beyond the task description. For
Sinhala-HAT we will report mean accuracy overall and by domain against a 50\%
chance rate; for Sinhala-ABD, mean sentence-level accuracy against the 63.6\%
majority-class rate, together with the proportion of boundaries identified
exactly. These sample sizes define an initial study and are not a guarantee of
statistical power. For comparison, annotators reached 0.770 accuracy on Reddit
in M4 \citep{wang2024m4}, and RoFT \citep{dugan2022roft} reports human
performance on locating a machine continuation.

\paragraph{Automatic validation.}
Both datasets apply automatic checks before a record is accepted, and no output
is edited by hand, so each corpus contains only text the generators actually
produced. Sinhala-HAT requires output within $\pm$10\% of the paired human word
count and free of preamble and Markdown; failures are regenerated twice, then
the source is discarded and replaced. Sinhala-ABD applies seven checks, since a
mixed document's labels are only correct if the generated span is usable in
place of the text it replaced: exact sentence count, word count within
$\pm$25\% of the replaced span, no preamble, no Markdown, predominantly Sinhala
script, not a near-copy of the original span, and preservation of numeric
tokens, so a date or quantity cannot silently mutate. Across 5{,}109 rejected
ABD generations, recording 5{,}951 check failures since one generation may fail
several, 53.7\% of failures were length violations, 23.9\% sentence-count
violations, and 16.3\% number mutations, with the remainder script violations,
near-copies, or formatting; 76.8\% of accepted documents passed on the first
attempt. Neither length tolerance was relaxed: generators systematically
under-write Sinhala, and admitting shorter machine text would let length
substitute for authorship as a detection signal
\citep{doughman2024limitations}.
